How to study

A little each day

Read two Learn cards. Try ten Quiz questions. Practice five spellings. Then write one explanation in your own words.

Learn

Read the facts from all five worksheet pages. Cover the Earth diagram labels to test yourself. Science notes and extra explanations are additions to your sheet.

Quiz

Tap the answer. The evidence question needs TWO choices, then Check both. Sort volcano facts into Composite, Shield, or Both. The highlighted layer on the diagram is the one to name. Wrong answers show a review card. Press Next when ready.

Spelling ★

Type the science word. Spelling counts. Capital letters, spaces, and incidental punctuation do not. After a miss, wrong letters are red. Retype the word correctly to continue. Practice full explanations separately.

Explain

Write an answer before opening the key ideas. Check each idea you included. Different words are fine if the meaning is right. Ask a parent to check your ideas AND spelling. These answers are not automatically graded and earn no stars. Drafts stay on this browser, even when you change tabs.

Stars

First try right: add one star. A miss: lose one star. Three stars means mastered. Weakest questions come first. After a round, use Practice these or Practice weak spots. Quiz and spelling have separate stars.

Added explanations and science notes

The guide uses short rewordings for concept practice, a redrawn layer diagram, and movement arrows. They are study aids, not extra worksheet facts. Explanations use equivalent wording such as move together for crash together and move apart for split apart.

The worksheet describes typical volcano patterns, not universal rules. Shield volcanoes can also form at divergent boundaries; not every eruption produces cinders; volcanic soil becomes fertile through weathering. Subduction depends on density, and an ocean plate can sink under another ocean plate. The asthenosphere is mostly solid, slowly flowing rock. Hess helped explain seafloor spreading; the discovery was not his alone.

For crust recycling, the important ideas are new crust forming at divergent boundaries and old crust returning to the mantle at subduction zones. Sinking plates do not simply melt and rise directly back up. Water from a sinking plate helps nearby mantle melt.
