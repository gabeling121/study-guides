How to practice

Ready for your Latin quiz?

1. Start with Written Answers: Latin to English. Read each Latin entry and type an English meaning from memory, without a word bank. Any listed English meaning counts. You can list meanings in any order, separated by a slash, comma, “or,” or “and.” For est, “she/he/it is” and “she is” both count. Common equivalents such as “dad” for “father” also count. Every meaning you include must be correct; missing or extra spaces are ignored; spelling still matters.

2. Play Match. Tap a Latin word and its English meaning.

3. Try Word Bank. Choose both directions, or focus on one. Tap the answer.

4. If you want to practice the other way, choose English to Latin or Both directions. Any one listed Latin form counts. Spelling matters; capitals do not.

Use the ā ē ī ō ū buttons to insert a macron at your cursor. Macrons are optional unless you turn on Require macrons before a round. Ask your parent which rule your teacher uses.

After a miss, read the correction and type an accepted answer to continue. Stars track both directions separately. Three stars means mastered. Matching rounds are just for fun and do not change stars.

Practice weak spots, then finish with a written round. Print a worksheet and answer key from Learn if you want paper practice.

The words and meanings come from your supplied Chapter 1 list. The original words and meanings are preserved in Learn. Written answers also accept these equivalents: apartment block or block of flats; select; dad or daddy; unsafe, risky, or perilous; male slave, enslaved man, or male enslaved person; throng; roadway.
