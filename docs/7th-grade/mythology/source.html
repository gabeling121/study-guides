Figure/storyAll supplied details
HerculesGreek hero noted for great strength; performed 12 labors during servitude to Eurystheus, King of Tiryns.
Hercules: 12 labors, in listed order1. Nemean Lion; 2. Lernean Hydra; 3. Cerynitian hind; 4. Erymanthian boar; 5. Augeian stables; 6. Stymphalian birds; 7. Cretan bull; 8. mares of Diomedes; 9. Hippolyte’s belt; 10. cattle of Geryon; 11. apples of the Hesperides; 12. Cerberus.
PerseusSon of Danae and Zeus. His grandfather feared Perseus would kill him and put mother and son into a box set into the sea. Zeus saved them. As an adult he first slew Medusa with Athena’s help, then the sea monster threatening Andromeda, who was chained to a rock as a sacrifice.
Orpheus and EurydiceOrpheus was mythology’s most famous musician. Eurydice, his wife, died from a snake bite. He went to the Underworld to bring her back, charmed the gods with music, and could lead her out only if he did not look back. He looked back and lost her.
Pyramus and ThisbeLovers whose parents opposed their marriage; their love led to their tragic deaths.
TheseusGreek hero; slew the half-man, half-bull Minotaur with help from Ariadne, daughter of King Minos of Crete. She told him to use a ball of string to escape the labyrinth afterward; he did. He then abandoned Ariadne and returned to Athens, his home.
AtalantaBeautiful young woman who would marry only a man who beat her in a running race. Many suitors tried and died as the condition of defeat. Hippomenes finally won when she stopped to pick up three golden apples he tossed during the race. Venus gave him the apples.
CirceGreat enchantress who could turn men into wild beasts. Changed Odysseus’s men into pigs on her island Aeaea. Hermes gave Odysseus the magic herb moly; Odysseus forced Circe to restore the men to human form.
PenelopeOdysseus’s wife; waited faithfully during his 20-year absence. Wove a shroud for her father-in-law Laertes. She was supposed to marry one of her many suitors upon finishing it; to postpone this, she unraveled at night what she wove by day.
NiobeBeautiful queen of Thebes; boasted she was superior to Leto, mother of Artemis and Apollo. Apollo killed her seven sons; Artemis killed her seven daughters. Niobe turned to stone while mourning her children.
Narcissus and EchoNarcissus was handsome and conceited. Echo was a beautiful nymph who loved him; he ignored her and loved his own reflection in a pool. He fell in while trying to embrace himself and died.
MidasKing of the Phrygians; restored Silenus, Dionysus’s teacher, to Dionysus. Dionysus offered a gift; Midas chose to turn everything he touched to gold. When he asked to be freed from this unwise choice, he was told to bathe in the river Pactolus. The power left him and passed to the river, whose sand became golden.
Creature/familyAll supplied details
Uranus and GaeaHeaven and earth; parents of the Hecatonchires, Cyclopes, and Titans.
Hecatonchires (Centimani)The sheet says Zeus imprisoned them in the bowels of the earth. Names: Briareos (also Aegaeon), Cottus, Gyes.
CyclopesThe sheet says Zeus had to bury them deep in the earth. Names: Brontes (thunder), Steropes (lightning), Arges (rapid flame). Predate Homer’s Cyclopes, who were sons of Poseidon, e.g. Polyphemus.
Oceanus and TyphonListed as Titans. Typhon had 100 dragon heads and a serpent-covered body.
Ceto and PhorcysListed as children of Oceanus by his mother Gaea. Produced the Graeae (dino, Enyo, Pephrido), Gorgons (Euryale, Stheno, Medusa), Sirens (Leucosia, Ligeia, Parthenope), Hesperides (Aegle, Arethusa, Erytheis), Scylla, Ladon, and Echidna.
EchidnaHalf-woman, half-snake; produced six monsters with her uncle Typhon.
ChimaeraTripartite monster with goat’s body, lion’s head, snake tail. Killed by Bellerophon riding Pegasus.
PegasusSprang from a drop of blood falling from Medusa’s severed head while Perseus carried it. Later kicked Mt. Helicon with his hoof; the Hippocrene Fountain sprang forth, sacred to the Muses and a source of poetic inspiration.
CerberusThree-headed dog guarding the Underworld entrance. Repeatedly tricked by visiting heroes with special dog treats, some drugged; the sheet describes these visits as dying and returning to life. Hercules brought him to the upper world as labor 12.
OrthrosTwo-headed brother of Cerberus; guarded Geryon’s oxen. Hercules killed him when stealing Geryon’s cattle as labor 10. Mated with his mother Echidna, or according to some accounts his sister Chimaera, producing the Sphinx and Nemean Lion.
SphinxWinged lion with woman’s head and bronze claws. Lived on a rock outside Thebes, killing passers-by unable to answer: “What walks on four legs in the morning, two at noon, and three at night?” Oedipus answered “A man”: a baby crawls, an adult walks on two legs, an older person uses a cane. The Sphinx jumped off the rock and killed herself.
Nemean LionImpenetrable hide. Hercules strangled it as labor 1, turned it inside out to clean it, and wore its hide afterward as his trademark.
GeryonTripartite monster depicted either as three bodies joined at the hips or three upper torsos on one lower body. Each head preferred particular foods. Hercules chased it to make it hungry, then placed its foods at opposite ends of a clearing. The sheet says it either tore itself apart trying to reach them or starved because each head refused to let another eat first.
Lernean HydraNine-headed water serpent. In labor 2, cutting off a head caused two to regrow from its neck. Hercules accepted forbidden help from charioteer Iolaus, who burned each neck with a torch before regrowth. The final head was immortal; Hercules buried it under a boulder.
VulturesCarrion birds, eating dead flesh; sacred to Mars/Ares because they clean battlefields after fighting. Their sacred status made them good omens for Romulus and Remus when competing to name their new city.
